A green gate proves nothing.
Until its twin goes red for the planted reason.

The essay makes the argument; this page is the evidence: dated, machine-checked rows of what a point-in-time gate found on a named fundamentals surface — and what it caught on a copy carrying one planted error. Every number here is read out of a row you can open, and prose the rows don't back wears a badge saying so. It reports on surfaces; it serves no data.

The claim path row-backed

The gate reads a fundamentals surface the way a consumer would, re-derives what should be visible at a chosen instant from the acceptance evidence, and counts disagreements. A green gate is credited only when the same gate, pointed at a copy of the surface carrying one deliberately planted error, goes red — catching exactly the plant and nothing else.

On 2026-09-01, the gate read 440,661 as-of rows of vantage-gold-local-parquet — scope cik % 97 == 0 sample, viewpoint d = 2024-07-01 — and found GREEN on all 3 checks. The same run against a copy with one planted error went RED.

How baselines fail — and the one that holds

Every fundamentals read surface handles the viewpoint problem in one of four ways. Three of them lose the claim — each in a different place — and each broken variant below is pinned to what this ledger actually holds against it.

00zero baseline
in the instrument

One viewpoint. Nothing shifts; a near star and the far background are indistinguishable. There is no measurement to get wrong.

on this ledger

commercial-fundamentals-api (APERTURE): NO_AS_OF_MODE NO_ACCEPTANCE_INSTANT across 48 documentation pages. The only date on a fundamentals record is filing_date — restatements overwrite in place, so lookahead has nowhere to show up and quietly becomes the dataset.

01unknown baseline
in the instrument

The shift is right there on the plate, but with no citable separation it never becomes a distance — a number with no meaning.

on this ledger

A green that has never been shown able to go red cites nothing, so the checker refuses it: a live cell arriving without its twin derives PARTIAL, never CLAIMABLE — a rule held by its own named control in test-ledger.mjs.

02flexing baseline
in the instrument

The separation moves between the two observations and the instrument manufactures shift — false parallax, indistinguishable from signal on a single pair of plates.

on this ledger

The twin exists to catch exactly this: plant_future_accepted moved the boundary by 1 row and the gate went RED with violations {1, 0, 1} — the plant, the whole plant, and nothing but the plant.

03stored viewpoint
in the instrument

Bessel's standard: the separation is surveyed and cited beside the value, so a better viewpoint later replaces without rewriting.

on this ledger

Lane 1 derives CLAIMABLE today: live GREEN over 440,661 as-of rows at d = 2024-07-01, twin red for the planted reason. One surface, one lane, one dated run — and no more than that.

What a feed without a viewpoint costs reported, not on the ledger

Measured on a sample of 17,787 filer-quarters from the same surface: when revisions land, they are rare but violent — in 1.51% of cases the reported growth story flips sign. A quarter your feed shows as growth was, on the day you would have traded it, a decline. This measurement is reported from PARALLAX study 001 and does not yet have a ledger row of its own; until it does, it carries this badge.
